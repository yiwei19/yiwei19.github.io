Publications

Fast Generative Grasping via Lie Group-Constrained MeanFlow
S. Talha Bukhari, Yi Wei, Ruiqi Ni, Zachary Kingston, Aniket Bera
arXiv preprint arXiv:2608.26076, August 2026

Grasp synthesis is multimodal by nature — a good grasp is a distribution, not a point estimate — which makes generative models a natural fit, but their iterative sampling is too slow for time-critical robot control. We formulate MeanFlow on the product Lie group SO(3) × ℝ3, coupling an algebraic semigroup consistency condition with Riemannian Conditional Flow Matching so that the average velocity stays anchored to the data distribution. The model samples reliable grasps in ≤ 5 network evaluations, matching state-of-the-art diffusion and flow-based grasp generators on ACRONYM at millisecond-scale latency (up to 39× speed-up), and transfers directly to real-world grasping with no additional training or domain adaptation.

My contribution: I owned the real-robot side — turning the model into a working stack on a physical Franka R3. I built the calibrated eye-in-hand RGB-D pipeline with an Orbbec Femto wrist camera, diagnosed and fixed a ~30 mm hand-eye error caused by a frame-convention mismatch, designed a shared-correction multi-view ICP that cut validation alignment error from 11.6 mm to 10.2 mm, wrote the adapter mapping generated SE(3) grasps into the robot frame, and integrated collision-aware VAMP planning — validating end-to-end execution to roughly 1 mm / 0.13°.
