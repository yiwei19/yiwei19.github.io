Publications

Fast Generative Grasping via Lie Group-Constrained MeanFlow
S. Talha Bukhari, Yi Wei, Ruiqi Ni, Zachary Kingston, Aniket Bera
arXiv preprint arXiv:2608.26076, August 2026

Grasp synthesis is multimodal by nature, which makes generative models a good fit — but their iterative sampling is too slow for time-critical robot control. We formulate MeanFlow on the product Lie group SO(3) × ℝ3, coupling an algebraic semigroup consistency condition with Riemannian Conditional Flow Matching. The resulting model samples reliable grasps in ≤ 5 network evaluations, matching state-of-the-art diffusion and flow-based grasp generators on ACRONYM at millisecond-scale latency (up to 39× speed-up), and transfers directly to real-world grasping with no additional training or domain adaptation.

Research & Projects

Real-Robot Deployment of SE(3) Generative Grasping

Purdue CoMMA Lab · 2026 · real-robot results in arXiv:2608.26076

I led the hardware side of the paper above: turning a grasp generation model into a working stack on a physical Franka R3. That meant calibrated eye-in-hand RGB-D perception with an Orbbec Femto wrist camera, hand-eye-aware multi-view fusion, language-prompted segmentation (LangSAM/SAM2), collision-aware VAMP planning, and execution on real hardware.

Concretely: diagnosed and fixed a ~30 mm hand-eye error caused by a frame-convention mismatch; designed a shared-correction multi-view ICP that cut validation alignment error from 11.6 mm to 10.2 mm; built the adapter mapping generated SE(3) grasps into the robot frame; and validated end-to-end execution to roughly 1 mm / 0.13°.
